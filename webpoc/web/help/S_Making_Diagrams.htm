Making a diagram (SED or PREDOM)

A diagram tab has the settings on the left and the diagram on the right (below the settings on a narrow screen). The settings are in four sections, which open and close with a click on their heading: Chemical system, Diagram, Concentrations and Options. When they are set, click Plot the diagram (Ctrl+Enter, or ⌘+Enter on a Mac).

Chemical system

The components and reactions of the data file, with the temperature and pressure it was made for. Here you can switch reactions off, change log K values, delete components and exchange them for reaction products. See changing the chemical system.

Diagram

Type. Only the types that the chemical system allows are offered:

Predominance area diagramPREDOMthe species that predominate for a main component, with a varied component on each axis. See predominance diagrams.
FractionsSEDthe fraction of a component in each of its species. See fraction diagrams.
Logarithmic concentrationsSEDlog of the concentrations of all species. See logarithmic diagrams.
Logarithmic solubilitiesSEDthe total dissolved concentration of each component; offered when the system has a solid or a gas. See solubility diagrams.
Relative activitiesSEDactivities relative to that of a reference species.
Calculated pHSEDthe pH of the solution; offered when the system has H+.
Calculated pe (or Eh)SEDthe redox potential; offered when the system has e−.
Logarithmic activities (advanced)SEDlog of the activities of all species.
H+ affinity spectrum (advanced)SEDsee proton affinity diagrams.

Components in the axes. Choose the X-axis component. A predominance diagram also needs a Y-axis component and a Main component, the one whose species are shown. A fraction diagram needs the component whose fractions are shown (Fractions of component), and a relative activity diagram a Reference component. A component placed in an axis gets a varied concentration, and a component taken out of the axes gets a fixed one.

The Y-axis has a row of its own at the end of the table of concentrations, marked as the row of the component in the X-axis is. It says what the axis shows (for example log concentration) and, for logarithmic, solubility, activity, pH and pe diagrams, has the range of the axis in the two fields. In a predominance diagram the Y-axis is a component, and its row is marked in the same way.

Show Eh instead of pe (for systems with e−) gives the redox potential in volts, in the concentrations, the axes and the Y range of a calculated Eh diagram. The temperature of the data file (or the one given in the Options) is used to convert pe to Eh. See Eh and pe.

Title (advanced): a text written at the top of the diagram. See how to enter texts.

Concentrations

One row for each component, with the type of concentration and its value, or its range when it is varied:

total conc.the total concentration in mol/kg (molal); for H+, the amount of acid added (negative for a base)
total conc. variedfrom … to … mol/kg
log total variedfrom … to …, as log of the total concentration
pH, pe, Eh (V)for H+ and e−: a fixed pH, pe or Eh
log Pfor a gas component, for example CO2(g): log of the partial pressure in bar
log activityfor other components: a fixed log activity
… variedthe same, varied from … to …

When the type changes between a fixed and a varied form of the same quantity (pH and pH varied, total conc. and total conc. varied, and so on), the value is kept: a range becomes its middle, and a value becomes the centre of a range. A type with other units starts from a default value.

The components in the axes must be varied, and the others fixed; a line below the table says what is missing. The data file keeps pH, pe, Eh and log P as log activities, as in the desktop programs, so files can be exchanged with them.

Options

In normal mode:

Ionic strengthempty or 0: no activity coefficients (ideal solution). A positive value: the activity coefficients for that fixed ionic strength. −1: the ionic strength is calculated at each point, assuming electroneutrality (Na+ or Cl− is added to balance the charge). See ionic strength.

In advanced mode also:

allow reversed axesnormally the lower value of a range is shown first. Tick this to keep a range in the order you give it, see concentrations.
Stepsthe number of calculation steps in each axis (default 50). More steps give smoother curves and sharper borders in predominance diagrams, and take longer.
Temperaturefor activity coefficients and Eh; the default is the temperature of the data file. The log K values are not changed. See temperature.
Activity coefficientsthe model: Davies, SIT or simplified HKF (the default). See activity coefficient models.
Diagram name (in the Diagram section)the name of the files you export (SVG, PNG, PDF, the table); empty for the name of the data file.
Tolerance in HaltaFall, tolerance for activity coefficientssee tolerance.
Concentration units, notation, text heighthow concentrations and texts are written in the diagram.
Tick step, X-axis and Y-axisthe step between the numbers of an axis, in its units; empty for the program's choice. Changes the drawing only, see the diagram.
Fraction thresholdSED: the smallest fraction for which a curve is drawn (default 0.03). Lower it to show minor species.
aqueous species only, neutral pH linePREDOM: leave out the solids (to show the aqueous speciation below them), and draw the line of neutral pH.
Table of resultsSED writes the values of the curves as a table; here you choose its name extension, column separator and comment characters. See table output.
write the table of resultsuntick it to make a SED diagram without the table. The species at the pointer are then not listed under the diagram, since they are read from the table.
Save as defaults, Program defaultsthe first keeps these options in this browser for the diagram tabs you open later, as the button of the desktop Spana; the second goes back to the options of the programs. The temperature, the title and the tick steps belong to a diagram and are not saved.
Debuggingmore output from SED, PREDOM and HaltaFall under Program messages.

Making the diagram

Plot the diagram writes the settings into the data file, saves it in this browser (as the desktop Spana saves the data file before it runs SED or PREDOM), and runs SED or PREDOM. The status line reports the number of equilibrium calculations; Stop interrupts a long calculation. If some points fail, or nothing is drawn, see Program messages below the diagram, and the programs fail?

Live mode

Tick Live, beside Plot the diagram, to have the diagram follow the settings: each change of the diagram type, an axis, a concentration or an option makes the diagram again, without a click on the button. The browser remembers the choice.

In live mode a slider is shown under each fixed value in the table of concentrations, so that you can see how the diagram changes with, for example, the total concentration of a metal or the partial pressure of CO2:

  • pH, pe, Eh, log P and log activity move between the limits of the quantity (pH 0 to 14, Eh −2 to 2 V);
  • a total concentration moves on a logarithmic scale, from 10−9 to 10 mol/kg;
  • a total concentration that is zero or negative, as it may be for H+ and e−, moves linearly around zero.

The slider writes its value in the table, and a value typed in the table moves the slider. The values of a varied component (the range of an axis) have no sliders; type them as usual.

In live mode the drawing keeps its place when the texts above it change. An ionic strength adds a line above the diagram, which would move the whole diagram down; live mode keeps room for that line from the start, so there is a little more space above the diagram than otherwise. The ionic strength is written on the new line, above the concentrations, and the temperature stays where it is. (The desktop programs, and the EPS, PS and plot files, have the two the other way round.)

Two options have sliders in live mode, under Options: the ionic strength, from 0 to 1 mol/kg, and in advanced mode the temperature of the calculation, from 0 to 100 °C. The temperature changes the activity coefficients and the Eh scale only. The log K values of a data file are those of the temperature it was made for in the Chemical system tab; for another temperature, search again with that temperature, see temperature.

The equilibrium constants have sliders too: open the Chemical system section, and each log K has a slider that moves three units to each side of the value the file was opened with, in steps of 0.05. A changed log K is shown in bold, and the button ↺ beside it takes it back to the value of the file. This shows, for example, how much a pKa or a solubility product must change before a diagram looks different. A changed log K is not part of the file that is saved for you when the diagram is made: see changing log K. To compare the diagrams before and after, keep the first one (see comparing diagrams).

Most diagrams are calculated in less than a tenth of a second, and then the diagram follows the slider while it is dragged. A slow calculation (many components, a calculated ionic strength, a high number of steps) is not started for every step of the slider: one calculation runs at a time, and the latest settings are used for the next one. While a setting is in error, nothing is calculated and the diagram is dimmed, as without live mode.

The data file is not saved for each of these diagrams. It is saved in this browser when the settings have been left unchanged for two seconds.

On a narrow screen (a phone) the diagram stays in the upper part of the tab in live mode, and the settings are scrolled under it.

The diagram and its export buttons are described in the diagram and exporting and printing.
